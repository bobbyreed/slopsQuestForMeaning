// 01 — who are they

what do we call them? the world's names lean plain and a little ominous — The Render, The Pixel, The Duplicate, The Prior — but yours doesn't have to.
be concrete. shape, size, how they move, what's wrong with them. chapter 1 is drawn in code from rectangles and circles, so simple readable shapes build better than intricate ones — but describe what you actually want and we'll find a way.
where did they come from, and what do they want? the game is about made things trying to work out what they're for, so characters who want something specific tend to fit — but it's your character. contradict us if you want.

// 03 — where do they live

you've just played the whole thing, so you know these places better than most people ever will. where does this character belong?
which screen, which corner, behind what. do they have to be looked for? can you walk past and never know? "in the room past the east wall that nobody from the cast talks about" is the level of specific we can build from.

// 04 — how does the fight start

every submitted character is an optional miniboss, so something has to make the fight happen. this is the most interesting decision on the form, so take it seriously: the way a fight begins says more about a character than the fight does.

if it's a dialogue tree, write the branches — what they say, what the player can answer, which answer opens the fight and which one closes the door. write actual lines if you have them; we'll use your words where we can.
optional but useful. what do they do, what makes them hard, what's the trick to beating them? if there's a way to end the encounter without winning, say so — this game likes those.
optional. do they die, leave, change, hand something over, keep standing there? do they remember it next time?
